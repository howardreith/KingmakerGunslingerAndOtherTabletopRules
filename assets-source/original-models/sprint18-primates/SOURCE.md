# Sprint 18 original primate bodies — Ape and Dire Ape

Everything in `assets/sprint18-primates/` is project-owned. The two meshes
and the two paintings were generated by the scripts in this directory and by
nothing else. No proprietary mesh, texture, animation curve or transform is
redistributed, and none is read: the only game-sourced input is a bind-pose
skeleton — bone names, bind positions and parent links — which the guarded
primate donor census captured read-only and which stays on the build machine.

## Why there was anything to author

The census (`observe-expanded-summoning-primate-census`, evidence
`20261009T1721285353210Z`) surveyed twenty-eight view prefabs out of two
hundred and eighty-eight discovered blueprints, read-only and detached, and
found **no primate unit type and no primate body** in the installed library.
There was nothing to clone, so both bodies had to be original geometry on a
non-primate rig.

Of the twenty-eight surveyed rigs it is the only Large one actually built like
an ape: an upright `Pelvis`-rooted spine, long two-segment arms ending in real
hands with a thumb and three fingers, a separate `Jaw_01`, and legs with toes.
It also already carries ape proportions — measured from the bind pose, the arm
from shoulder to hand is 2.06 units against 1.72 from hip to ankle, so the arms
are longer than the legs and the hands hang below the knees.

Rejected, with the reason: the Owlbear both apes rode provisionally (29 bones,
no hands, no jaw, no toes, a quadruped bear); the Athach (64 bones but Huge and
equally handless); the Cyclops and the giants (48-bone upright humanoids with a
human gait and no ape arm length); the UMA human rigs.

## What the two bodies are

One rig, one generator, two creatures separated by geometry rather than by rig.

| | Ape (Gorilla) | Dire Ape (Gigantopithecus) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertices / triangles | 2720 / 3146 | 3552 / 4106 |
| Closed shells | 51 | 67 |
| Driver bones | 56 | 56 |
| Max influences per vertex | 2 | 2 |
| Bulk | 1.00 | 1.14 |
| Sagittal crest | low | tall |
| Shoulder collar | none | shaggy ruff |
| Canines | hidden inside the lip | long and showing |
| Hands | nails | heavy curved claws |
| Coat | near-black with a silver saddle | russet brown with a straw mane |

The feature list is not decoration: the Dire Ape bites for 1d6 and rakes for
two primary 1d4 claws, so it has a jaw that opens and claws that are visible;
the Ape has two slams and no bite and no claws, so it has neither.

## The bones that carry geometry, and the five that do not

Fifty-six of the sixty-one body-skin bones carry geometry: the spine, stomach,
neck, head, jaw, the brow, lip and ear bones on both sides, and the full arm,
hand, five-digit and leg chains on both sides.

Five carry none, for a stated reason rather than by oversight: `Tail_01` and
`Tail_02`, because an ape has no tail, and `Tongue_01` through `Tongue_03`,
because neither printed routine has a tongue attack. The generator, the
offline fixtures, the runtime policy and the repository validator each refuse a
mesh that weights one of them.

## Honest limitations

- **The gait is the donor's.** Kingmaker plays the Troll guard's clips, which
  are a large upright biped, not a knuckle-walker. The geometry is an ape; the
  walk, run and strike timing are the donor's. Authoring a primate gait would
  be a general animation system, which this sprint does not build.
- **Nothing here is runtime qualified.** The review renders are synthetic
  offline poses. They prove the geometry holds together when the donor joints
  move and that the silhouette reads at party-camera distance. They prove no
  timing, no contact point and no in-game behaviour.
- **Fur is painted, not simulated.** The silhouette carries the shaggy mass;
  the strands are in the albedo.

## Triangle winding

These use the shared exporter convention, marked `shared-exporter-sprint18`:
geometry is authored in the donor bind frame, Blender orients each closed shell
outward in its own right-handed frame, and the exporter reverses the winding
once for the engine's left-handed frame. That is the same convention as the
Pteranodon, flying-animal, ungulate, Sprint 12, Sprint 13, Sprint 14 and
Sprint 16 bodies.

This is not taken on trust. The generator computes the signed volume of every
connected shell and refuses to export if any one of them is negative, and
`test_primate_prototype.py` repeats that check independently. Sprint 17 needed
the opposite convention for its coiled, self-intersecting snake bodies; these
bodies are checked rather than assumed, and they come out the other way.

## Reproduction

Use the installed Blender in background mode with `--python-exit-code 1`.
PRIVATE_CAPTURE is the census evidence file named above; it is not in this
repository. For each key `ape` and `dire-ape`:

1. `paint_primate_albedo.py -- --kind KEY --out PRIVATE/KEY-albedo.png`
2. `generate_primates.py -- --kind KEY --capture PRIVATE_CAPTURE
   --albedo PRIVATE/KEY-albedo.png --mesh-data PRIVATE/KEY-mesh.json
   --report PRIVATE/KEY-report.json --blend-out PRIVATE/KEY.blend`
3. `render_primate_review.py -- --blend PRIVATE/KEY.blend
   --out-dir PRIVATE/review --suite --resolution 420`
4. `test_primate_prototype.py -- --capture PRIVATE_CAPTURE`

Step 3 writes twenty-four private review sheets per creature: clay,
silhouette, textured and unlit at rest from four angles; the forward slam, the
synthetic contact frame, the one-armed claw rake and the crouch; a head
close-up with the jaw open; and a hand close-up with the fingers curled.

`HumanReview: NOT_PERFORMED_NONBLOCKING`.
